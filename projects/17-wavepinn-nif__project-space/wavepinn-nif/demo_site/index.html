Why It Matters

Why Use the PINN

The PINN is not there to decorate the project. It turns the wave dynamics into a compact learned surrogate that can be sampled continuously, compared against the reference field, and presented as a convincing physical surface demo.

Continuous spatiotemporal field

The learned model represents u(x, z, t) directly, so the field can be sampled on demand instead of being limited to one fixed discrete export.

Compact surrogate

A single checkpoint stands in for the evolving wavefield and can drive validation plots, offline renders, and future interactive tooling from the same representation.

Coherent approximation

The fit is strong enough to preserve recognizable propagation structure, which makes the learned result read as a field approximation rather than decorative motion.

Better presentation leverage

Once the field is learned, it can be remapped onto the fixed-topology Taichi surface and presented as a clean native demo instead of a brittle export path.
